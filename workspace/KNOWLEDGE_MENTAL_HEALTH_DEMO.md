# Office Knowledge: Willowmere Behavioral Health
Schema: abita-office-knowledge/v1

## Emergency and Urgency
Status: available

Willowmere provides scheduled outpatient care rather than emergency or crisis response. If someone may act on thoughts of suicide or harming someone, has made an attempt, cannot stay safe, faces an overdose, or has another life-threatening emergency, direct them to call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department now. In the United States, they may also call or text 988 for the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. Immediate danger exits the routine office workflow; an appointment, transfer, staff message, or callback does not replace emergency or crisis help.

For emotional distress or a medication or clinical concern without immediate danger, trained staff must assess the next clinical step. The receptionist can route the caller but does not diagnose, provide therapy, interpret symptoms, decide clinical urgency, or give medication advice.

## Scope of Services
Status: available

### New-Patient Therapy

Willowmere represents outpatient individual, couples, family, and group therapy for adults, adolescents, and school-age children. New-patient requests begin with an administrative intake for service, age group, location or telehealth preference, scheduling preference, and payment path. The receptionist does not ask for a detailed trauma narrative or determine diagnosis or clinical fit.

### Trauma and PTSD Care

The demo represents trauma-informed therapy and clinician-delivered modalities that may include cognitive behavioral therapy, cognitive processing therapy, prolonged exposure therapy, eye movement desensitization and reprocessing, often called EMDR, trauma-focused cognitive behavioral therapy, and skills-based stabilization. A licensed clinician determines whether any modality is appropriate. Availability varies by age, provider, location, telehealth eligibility, and clinical fit.

### Psychiatry and Medication Management

Oakview and Riverbend represent psychiatric evaluations and medication-management follow-ups. Clinicians assess diagnoses, treatment options, prescriptions, monitoring, and medication changes. The receptionist handles administrative requests and routes clinical questions without offering medication advice.

### Other Outpatient Services

Riverbend represents outpatient substance-use assessment and treatment and a structured higher-support outpatient program. Oakview represents psychological testing for concerns such as attention, learning, mood, and diagnostic clarification. These services require staff review for fit, prerequisites, and current availability. The demo does not represent inpatient hospitalization, residential treatment, detoxification, or emergency services.

### Existing Appointments, Referrals, Records, Forms, and Portal

The receptionist can help an existing patient identify whether they need to confirm, cancel, reschedule, receive location or telehealth instructions, or send another appointment request. Referrals may be sent by a clinician, school, employer assistance program, court, or the caller, but receipt and sufficiency require staff confirmation.

Medical-record copies, releases of information, disability or leave forms, school forms, intake paperwork, and portal access are handled by staff. Portal support includes invitation, sign-in, password-reset, missing-form, and telehealth-link requests. The receptionist never claims a referral, form, record request, portal change, or link is complete without the owning system or staff confirmation.

### Prescription and Clinical Concerns

Routine refill, pharmacy-change, medication prior-authorization, and prescription-status requests can be sent to staff for review. A request does not guarantee approval, prescribing, completion, or timing. Side effects, medication reactions, missed doses with possible clinical risk, dose questions, requests to start, stop, hold, combine, or change medication, symptom interpretation, treatment questions, and test results require trained clinical staff. Emergency guidance comes first when immediate danger may be present.
