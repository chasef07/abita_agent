## Scope of Services
Status: available

Juniper Ridge evaluates and manages rheumatic and musculoskeletal conditions including rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis, lupus, psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, gout, osteoporosis, vasculitis, myositis, scleroderma, Sjogren disease, fibromyalgia, bursitis, tendonitis, and persistent joint or muscle pain.

Evaluation may include a detailed history, joint and musculoskeletal examination, laboratory testing, X-ray imaging, and diagnostic ultrasound when ordered by a clinician. One visit may begin an evaluation without producing a final diagnosis.

Treatment is individualized and may include clinician-directed medication, physical or occupational therapy referrals, bracing, joint injections, and infusion therapy. Infusions, injections, and procedure visits require clinical review, any needed insurance authorization, and staff scheduling.

The demo can book returned medical appointment slots for rheumatology care. Infusion, injection, imaging, laboratory, and procedure coordination is handled through a staff request.

### General Medication Education

Disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs, often called DMARDs, are prescription treatments used to manage the underlying inflammatory disease rather than only relieve a symptom. Conventional DMARDs used in rheumatology include methotrexate, hydroxychloroquine, sulfasalazine, and leflunomide.

Methotrexate is a conventional disease-modifying antirheumatic drug used for several inflammatory rheumatic conditions. Improvement may take several weeks, and regular laboratory monitoring is commonly required. A caller's exact dose, schedule, side effects, infection plan, pregnancy plan, and decision to hold or stop it belong to the prescribing clinician.

Biologics target specific parts of the immune system involved in inflammation. Depending on the medication, they may be given by injection or infusion and may require infection screening, insurance authorization, and ongoing laboratory or clinical monitoring.

Hydroxychloroquine is a conventional DMARD used for conditions such as lupus and rheumatoid arthritis. Prednisone is a corticosteroid that reduces inflammation. NSAIDs are anti-inflammatory medicines used for pain and inflammation. Personal suitability, dosing, combinations, monitoring, and medication changes belong to the clinician who knows the patient's health history.

Some rheumatology treatment plans combine medicines from different classes, but the safe combination depends on the patient's diagnoses, other prescriptions, laboratory results, infection risk, allergies, pregnancy plans, and other health conditions. The assistant may explain that combination therapy exists while leaving the caller's actual combination to a clinician or pharmacist with the complete medication list.

A clinician may consider holding a medication around an infection, surgery, procedure, vaccine, pregnancy, or a significant side effect. The exact medication and timing matter, so the assistant may explain why a hold question comes up while leaving the caller's stop, hold, and restart instructions to the prescribing clinician.

These facts are approved for general education rather than personal medication advice. When a caller asks what they personally can take, whether two medicines can be combined, or whether they should change treatment, connect them with clinical staff.

## What to Bring
Status: available

Bring photo identification, an insurance card, any required referral, completed forms, and a complete medication list covering prescriptions, over-the-counter medicines, vitamins, herbs, and supplements. Include medication allergies and the pharmacy used for prescriptions.

Bring relevant primary-care or specialist notes, prior rheumatology records, recent laboratory results, imaging reports or discs, and a brief symptom history. Note which joints or body areas are affected, when symptoms began, and whether swelling, stiffness, rash, fever, fatigue, or other changes occur.

## Appointment Expectations
Status: available

A first rheumatology visit usually includes a detailed medical and family history, medication review, symptom discussion, and physical examination. A clinician may order laboratory testing or imaging and schedule follow-up before confirming a diagnosis or treatment plan.

Medication changes, injections, infusions, and other treatments follow clinician evaluation. Insurance authorization or additional testing may be required before treatment starts.

The demo directly schedules rheumatology consultations and follow-ups. Staff handles infusion, injection, procedure, laboratory, imaging, referral, medication, and authorization coordination.
